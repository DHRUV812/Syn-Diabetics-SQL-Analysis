# Diabetes Complications, Treatment & Risk Analysis (SQL)

[![Open In Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/assets/colab-badge.svg)](https://colab.research.google.com/github/DHRUV812/Syn-Diabetics-SQL-Analysis/blob/main/Syn_Diabetics_SQL_Analysis.ipynb)

A SQL analysis of longitudinal patient records for a cohort of patients with type 2 diabetes. It maps complications, compares HbA1c and complication burden across treatment groups, and flags patients for targeted clinical action, mirroring the care-pathway analytics that payers and health systems use to manage chronic disease.

> **All data is synthetic.** It was generated by [Synthea](https://synthea.mitre.org/), so no real patients are involved. This project demonstrates method; its results are not clinical evidence.

## Questions

1. **Cohort:** who are the patients with type 2 diabetes (age at diagnosis, sex, alive or deceased)?
2. **Complications:** which complications and comorbidities does each patient have (obesity, kidney disease, neuropathy, retinopathy)?
3. **Treatment:** which patients are on metformin, insulin, both, or neither?
4. **Comparison:** how do HbA1c and complication burden differ across treatment groups?
5. **Risk and action:** which patients need intervention, and who should be prioritized for diabetes prevention?

## Data

Synthea's synthetic sample dataset: 98 patients, 12 of them with type 2 diabetes. Four tables are linked by patient ID:

| Table | Used for |
|---|---|
| `patients` | Demographics, birth and death dates |
| `conditions` | Diagnoses with onset dates |
| `medications` | Prescriptions with start dates and reasons |
| `observations` | Lab results, including HbA1c (LOINC 4548-4) |

## Approach

1. **Data validation:** checked clinical congruence before any analysis.
2. **Cohort profile:** demographics and age at diagnosis.
3. **Patient-level flags:** SQL views turn diagnoses and prescriptions into one row per patient, with complication and treatment flags.
4. **Comparison:** average HbA1c and complications per patient, by treatment group.
5. **Risk flags:** clinical rules translated into SQL logic that assigns urgent and ongoing actions to each patient.
6. **Prevention cohort:** patients with prediabetes at the highest risk of progressing.

**Tools:** SQL (DuckDB), Python (pandas, matplotlib), Google Colab

## Key findings

### Data validation
Several records would fail clinical review, which is why the data was checked before being analyzed:
- **Unchanging HbA1c:** 5 of 12 patients with diabetes have the same HbA1c value at every test for years, including one patient at 3.8%.
- **Insulin coded for prediabetes:** 2 patients have insulin prescribed with prediabetes recorded as the reason.
- **Short record window:** records cover roughly the last 10 years per patient, even for diagnoses made decades earlier.

### Treatment comparison

| Treatment group | Patients | Avg HbA1c | Avg complications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metformin + insulin | 2 | 5.2% | 2.5 |
| Metformin only | 6 | 5.9% | 1.7 |
| Insulin only | 1 | 3.8% | 1.0 |
| No diabetes drug | 3 | 6.7% | 0.7 |

- **Complication burden rises with treatment intensity,** from 0.7 per untreated patient to 2.5 on combination therapy. That's consistent with treatment escalating as the disease advances.
- **Kidney involvement is concentrated among insulin users:** 3 of 3 patients on insulin, against 1 of 6 on metformin alone. That's consistent with metformin being restricted as kidney function declines.
- **The untreated group has the highest average HbA1c,** at 6.7%.

### Risk flags and prevention
- **6 of 9 living patients with diabetes** have at least one urgent action, and **7 of 9** are obese, which puts them in a weight-management pathway.
- **Among 31 living patients with prediabetes,** 21 are in the high-risk HbA1c band (6.0–6.4%), and **14 of those are also obese.** They are the priority cohort for a prevention program.

## Risk flag rules

| Flag | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| No diabetes drug | Latest HbA1c ≥ 6.5% and no metformin or insulin | Start or confirm treatment |
| Hypoglycemia risk | On insulin and latest HbA1c < 6.0% | Review hypoglycemia risk |
| Kidney involvement | Diabetic kidney disease, CKD, albuminuria or proteinuria | Kidney monitoring and dose review |
| Overdue test | More than 183 days since the last HbA1c | HbA1c overdue |
| Ongoing care | Obesity / neuropathy / retinopathy | Weight management / foot care / eye follow-up |

**Prevention cohort:** prediabetes, no diabetes diagnosis, alive, and a latest HbA1c of 6.0–6.4%, with obesity shown as a priority flag.

Days since the last test are counted from the last date in the dataset, so results don't depend on the day the notebook is run.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data:** HbA1c values follow the simulator's rules, including abrupt step changes and values that stay flat for years.
- **Small cohort:** with 12 patients with diabetes, all comparisons are descriptive. Nothing here is statistically or causally conclusive.
- **Limited history:** with about 10 years of records per patient, medication start dates at the beginning of the window may not be true start dates.
